  • Host: Welcome back to the show! Today we're talking about an article on Thanksgiving weekend in the U.S. Did you get a chance to read it?  
  • Guest: I did, and honestly, it made me hungry. And a little homesick, too.  
  • Host: Same here. What surprised me first was the travel. People drive eight hours just to catch up with their families.  
  • Guest: Oh, totally. I spent half my childhood on the road every November. My dad would sit in bumper-to-bumper traffic for hours and never complain once. Well, almost never.  
  • Host: And then you finally get there, and the house is packed with people.  
  • Guest: Right, but my grandma always said, "The more, the merrier." She'd invite neighbors, coworkers, anyone who didn't have somewhere to go.  
  • Host: I love that. And the food! Turkey, stuffing, three kinds of pie. Families really go all out.  
  • Guest: They do. In my family, nobody was allowed to dig in until Grandpa finished his speech. And believe me, it was a long speech.  
  • Host: So, do you save room for dessert, or are you a "turkey first, worry later" kind of person?  
  • Guest: I always try to save some space, but I fail every single year. Your eyes are bigger than your stomach on Thanksgiving.  
  • Host: That's why everyone ends up in a food coma in front of the football game!  
  • Guest: Guilty. Although my favorite part is Friday morning, when you polish off the pie for breakfast.  
  • Host: Pie for breakfast. Now that's a tradition I can support.  
  • Guest: What about Black Friday? Have you ever gone shopping at five in the morning?  
  • Host: Once. Never again. Standing outside in the cold with a thousand bargain hunters just isn't my cup of tea.  
  • Guest: I'm with you. I'd rather sleep in and shop online in my pajamas.  
  • Host: Agree. My family usually skips the mall completely. We go see a movie, walk around a little town nearby, and just take it easy.  
  • Guest: Honestly, that sounds perfect. No alarm clock, no long lines, and more pie.  
  • Host: Smart move. The article also says that this is the weekend when families kick off the Holiday season. You know, trees, lights, music in every shop.  
  • Guest: Oh, my mom would put on Christmas songs the second the dishes were done. She said it helped everyone get into the holiday spirit.  
  • Host: And then by Sunday night, it's time to wind down and get ready for work again.  
  • Guest: With a suitcase full of turkey sandwiches, of course.  
  • Host: Of course! Well, that's all for today. Happy Thanksgiving, everyone, and thanks for listening!
