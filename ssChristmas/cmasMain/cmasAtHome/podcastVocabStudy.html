Vocabulary Study Guide

Click on each word to see its definition and example

get into the spirit
Definition: (expression) To start to feel happy and excited about a holiday or special event.
Example: Hanging up the lights always helps me get into the spirit of the season.
go all out
Definition: (idiom) To use a lot of effort, energy, or money to do something as well as possible.
Example: For her son's tenth birthday, she decided to go all out with balloons, a magician, and a huge cake.
drag out
Definition: (phrasal verb) To pull or carry something heavy out of the place where it is kept.
Example: Every summer, we drag out the old fan from the basement.
a pain in the neck
Definition: (idiom, informal) Something or someone that is very annoying.
Example: Finding a parking space downtown is a pain in the neck.
put off
Definition: (phrasal verb) To delay doing something until a later time.
Example: Don't put off your homework until Sunday night.
cut corners
Definition: (idiom) To do something in the quickest or cheapest way, often by skipping steps.
Example: The builders tried to cut corners, and now the roof leaks.
make a mess
Definition: (collocation) To make a place dirty or untidy.
Example: Please don't make a mess in the kitchen while I'm out.
sweet tooth
Definition: (idiom) A strong liking for sugary foods such as candy and cake.
Example: My grandfather has a real sweet tooth and always keeps chocolate in his pocket.
sneak a peek
Definition: (expression) To look at something quickly and secretly, often when you are not supposed to.
Example: She tried to sneak a peek at the last page of the mystery novel.
stay up late
Definition: (collocation) To go to bed later than usual.
Example: We decided to stay up late and watch the meteor shower.
wide awake
Definition: (collocation) Completely awake and alert, not sleepy at all.
Example: After that strong coffee, I was wide awake until midnight.
tear into
Definition: (phrasal verb, informal) To start opening, eating, or doing something quickly and with a lot of energy.
Example: The kids always tear into the pizza as soon as it arrives.
pig out
Definition: (phrasal verb, informal) To eat a very large amount of food.
Example: We usually pig out on popcorn and candy during movie nights.
at the end of the day
Definition: (idiom) When everything is considered; what matters most in the end.
Example: Grades are important, but at the end of the day, you should enjoy what you study.
wrap up
Definition: (phrasal verb) To finish something such as a meeting, show, or discussion.
Example: Let's wrap up the meeting so everyone can go home.
